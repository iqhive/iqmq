    memory¶

    Maturity: Experimental · Tier: 1 · Owner: iqmq maintainers

    the driver documentation delegates this page, and the specification describes the engine. What follows is the driver over that engine: what a caller of the public API sees, and where this driver's answer differs from a network broker's.

    A memory:// DSN names a broker inside one process, and two processes that open the same DSN do not meet. The scheme looks like every other driver's, so it is worth saying plainly: there is no socket, no port and no address, and the string after memory:// selects a broker within this program rather than a place to reach. Two goroutines share one; two processes -- a producer and a consumer started separately, or a test binary and the program under test -- each get their own and neither sees the other's messages.

    Client & version policy¶

    There is no client library and no broker. The driver is a github.com/iqhive/iqmq.Backend over internal/engine, in the same module as the root package, so it is always available and always the same version as the core. Client.Info() reports Name: "memory", a ClientName naming the engine it wraps, and an empty BrokerVersion — the specification makes an empty string "the driver did not say" rather than a claim, and there is no broker here to report one.

    Nothing is persistent. The specification forbids this driver from advertising durable storage and it does not: FeatureDurableStorage and FeatureDurableConfirm are unsupported, and the two guarantees that are real but die with the process — a subscription outliving its consumers, and deduplication inside a window — are advertised as EmulatedVolatile, the one level the specification forbids from satisfying a durability requirement.

    Topic / subscription mapping¶

    A topic is a topic and a subscription is a subscription; nothing is mapped onto anything.

    iqmq memory
    topic engine.Topic, one per name, created through the admin surface or by AutoCreate
    subscription engine.Subscription, one independent consumer group per name
    consumer engine.Consumer, attached to a subscription; consumers of one subscription compete
    partition one lane per subscription-partition, TopologyConfig.Partitions of them

    AutoCreate is false by default, which is what every network driver does and what the specification requires of Open: a publisher or a consumer naming an unknown topic fails with ErrTopicNotFound. NewForTest turns it on, and Admin.EnsureTopic is the explicit route.

    Topic names are held to the portable grammar mq.ValidateTopic defines. The driver adds no restriction of its own.

    Publish path & confirmation boundary¶

    Publish validates, copies the caller's storage once, hashes the key to a lane, reserves capacity in every target subscription in a stable order and enqueues. It returns when the record is in every lane the fan-out policy made it wait for.

    That is the whole of the confirmation this driver can offer, and it is what each mode means here:

    Mode Boundary
    ConfirmNone the same as ConfirmAccepted: there is no local admission step separate from the lane
    ConfirmAccepted (default) the record is in every target lane and a consumer can claim it
    ConfirmDurable fails at construction with ErrUnsupported

    ConfirmDurable fails when the publisher is opened, not when it publishes: there is no durable boundary to define, and the specification requires the refusal to be at construction.

    PublishBatch reaches the engine's own batch call. The result is index-aligned with the caller's slice: the engine's batch stops at the first element it refuses and names its index, and the driver records that element's outcome and offers the rest, so a refusal is never reported against the messages behind it. Under FanoutIndependent a batch whose messages all reached at least one subscription answers ErrPartial when some of them missed one. Once a message of the batch is refused outright the answer is a *mq.BatchError, and every message that reached only some subscriptions carries ErrPartial in its own entry, which is not retryable.

    Three publication surfaces ship:

    Surface Contract
    Sink.Publish (default) safe copy: the message is cloned on admission and the caller may reuse its Key, Body and header storage the instant Publish returns
    OwnedSink.PublishOwned the storage transfers to the engine and Release runs exactly once — on settlement, on rejection, on a refused option and on close
    AsyncSink.PublishAsync safe copy, as Publish makes it: the engine admits synchronously, so the completion callback reports the admission (nil, or ErrPartial for a partial fan-out) before PublishAsync returns, and the storage is the caller's again once it has run

    FlushSink.Flush is implemented and has nothing to do: the engine admits synchronously, so a flush is a barrier that has already passed. It is implemented rather than omitted so that the boundary is stated here instead of inferred from a missing method. The asynchronous callback reports admission and not consumption, so a Flush never waits for a consumer, and a Close finds no publication outstanding to report as SettlementUnknown.

    Under OverflowBlock, the default, a publication into a full lane parks until there is room. Closing the publisher, or the client, ends that wait at once and the publication returns ErrClosed. Nothing was admitted, so the outcome is definite: an owned payload is released and an asynchronous callback does not run. Deleting the full subscription it waits on (Admin.DeleteSubscription) ends the wait too, and the publication goes to the subscriptions the topic still has, even when the delete lands as the publication starts to wait. With none left it is discarded, like any publication to a topic with no subscription.

    Consume path¶

    Source.Fetch fills the caller's slice from the consumer's assigned lanes, rotating the start index so that no lane starves. It blocks only when nothing is available, and it allocates nothing per fetch and nothing per message: the claims come out of a buffer allocated once when the source opens, and the engine handle a settlement needs later is a pointer in DriverDelivery.Ref's interface word rather than a boxed value.

    Source.Close unblocks a parked Fetch (the obligation), which is why the core never has to merge the caller's context with a cancellation of its own — a merge costs two or three allocations on the one path that may not have them.

    DeliveryInfo is filled as follows. The Has flags matter: the specification forbids "the broker did not say" from being the same answer as zero.

    Field Memory
    Topic the resolved topic; on a wildcard topic the publication's subject rather than the pattern
    Source always empty: there is no broker-side origin behind a memory topic
    Subscription the subscription the source was opened on
    ReceivedAt the broker clock at the fetch
    LeaseExpires ReceivedAt plus the subscription's lease (the deadline it was created with, whatever a later source asks for); the zero time when that lease is negative, which is "leases never expire"
    Attempt the delivery count, 1 on a first delivery
    Redelivered Attempt > 1
    Partition / HasPartition the lane; always set
    Sequence / HasSequence the record's publication sequence within its topic; always set
    Offset / HasOffset never set: the engine has no per-partition offset distinct from the sequence

    Delivery.Native() is nil. The engine's envelope is opaque by contract and a caller could not name its type, so surfacing it would be an escape hatch that escapes to nothing. Client.Native() returns the *memory.Broker, which is the escape hatch that is worth having.

    A received Body, Key and header value alias the engine's own storage and are valid until the delivery is settled, which is the safe receive mode. Nothing is recycled unless ConsumerConfig.BufferReuse is set. With ZeroOnRelease the engine overwrites the payload as it releases the record, so a delivery read after it was settled sees zeros rather than its data.

    The source implements DeadlineExtender and deliberately not BatchSettler: the engine settles one delivery at a time under one lane mutex, so a batch method would be the loop the core already writes, and a driver that offers one makes the core skip its own adapter for no saving.

    Ack / nack / extend mapping¶

    Verb Native action
    Ack terminal: the record leaves the subscription and its payload is released once every subscription has settled it
    Nack (requeue) the record returns to its lane, immediately or through the delayed structure when a delay is named; the attempt count survives and the next delivery increments it
    Nack(NackDrop()) the engine's terminal settlement — the same native action an acknowledgement takes
    Reject / Nack(NackDeadLetter()) publish to the topology's DeadLetterTopic, then acknowledge
    Extend the lease deadline moves to at least by from now, through the broker's shared lease scheduler. A lease with longer left keeps its deadline, and one that never expires (AckDeadline -1) keeps never expiring

    Two of those need a sentence each.

    Drop is an acknowledgement to the engine. The engine has one terminal settlement, so a dropped record is counted in Snapshot.Acked rather than in Snapshot.Dropped, and Snapshot.Dropped keeps its own meaning: a record discarded without a settlement. Most brokers are the same — a delete or an offset commit serves both verbs — and the distinction iqmq keeps is the consumer's own: ConsumerHandle.Stats() counts a drop as a negative settlement.

    A settlement carries the generation the delivery was claimed under, so a stale one is answered rather than applied to the redelivery that took its place: a lease that expired or was rebalanced away reports ErrLeaseLost, and a consumer that closed reports ErrClosed.

    Ordering¶

    This is the sentence delegates to each driver page.

    The memory driver delivers one ordered lane per subscription-partition, in publication sequence. With the default Partitions: 1 that is one ordered lane per subscription, which is the guarantee without the caller asking for it. A topic with more than one partition orders each lane and not the topic; a key always hashes to the same lane, so one key's records are ordered with respect to each other.

    What may overtake is a redelivery. Under the default OrderingRelaxed a record that comes back from a negative acknowledgement, a lease expiry or a consumer close is placed by the retry-lane kind (pushfront by default), which serves it before the records still waiting — the redelivery-first order real brokers use. OrderingStrict stops it: the redelivery blocks its lane, and nothing published behind it is delivered until it has been settled or routed to a dead-letter topic.

    Three things carry a caveat of their own, and all three are control operations rather than delivery:

    Reorder (the fault) never fires on a lane whose topic asked for strict ordering, nor on an engine whose kinds order what they hold.

    Delays¶

    Native, both forms. Delay and DeliverAt hold a publication back; a negative acknowledgement with NackDelay holds a redelivery back; TTL discards a record that has still not been delivered that long after it became due.

    All three are driven by one scheduler per broker, which sleeps until the next non-empty slot: there is no timer and no goroutine per message. The granularity is the selected EngineConfig.Timer kind's: Broker.SchedulerResolution() reports it, the wheels quantise to a millisecond and the heaps fire at the exact due time. A Config.SchedulerResolution finer than the selected kind provides is refused at construction rather than silently approximated.

    Time moves by itself on the wall clock and only on demand under a TestClock: Broker.Advance moves the clock and runs the sweeps the move implies, which is why TestClock has no Advance of its own. Inside a testing/synctest bubble the wall clock follows the bubble clock, so a test needs neither.

    Dead-letter queue¶

    There is no implicit dead-letter topic: TopologyConfig.DeadLetterTopic names one and it must already exist, because the engine never creates a topic behind the operator's back. A record whose attempts run out with no usable destination is discarded and counted in Snapshot.Dropped.

    Two routes reach it, and they are accounted differently:

    • The attempt budget. A failed delivery past MaxAttempts is published to the dead-letter topic by the engine and counted in Snapshot.DeadLettered. A publication that fails returns the record to its lane and is counted in Snapshot.DeadLetterFailures — it never acknowledges.
    • An explicit Reject. The driver publishes and then acknowledges, so the record is counted in Snapshot.Acked on the source subscription. See "Ack / nack / extend mapping".

    Neither route waits for room. Where the dead-letter topic's overflow policy would park a publisher (OverflowBlock, the default), the copy is refused instead, so a full dead-letter topic nobody drains cannot hold up the handlers that reject. The engine's route returns the record to its lane, and a refused Reject returns a retryable ErrQueueFull (CodeResourceExhausted) and leaves the delivery unsettled. A copy the dead-letter topic could never hold is refused with an ErrQueueFull that is not retryable.

    Under FanoutIndependent a dead-letter topic with several subscriptions can take the copy into some and not others. Both routes count that partial fan-out as published and settle the record: the subscriptions with room hold the copy, and the full one counts the refusal in Snapshot.Rejected. A Reject that failed on it instead would be retried and add a second copy to every subscription that took the first.

    A rebalance never dead-letters: a revoked delivery is not a failed one, so it does not consume an attempt.

    Headers & limits¶

    Every piece of metadata is native. The engine stores the *mq.Message itself, so binary header values, duplicate keys, insertion order, the identifier and every typed property travel unchanged, and nothing is ever unsupported metadata. Three of the four modes therefore come out identical here: MetadataNativeStrict has nothing to reject, MetadataAdaptive never needs the envelope, and MetadataLenientDrop never drops anything.

    MetadataPortableEnvelope is honoured as written, because the whole point is that the envelope is never applied by accident and never skipped when it was asked for. A sink in that mode wraps the message in the iqmq/wire envelope and publishes the envelope as the whole body; a source in the same mode decodes it. A source in native mode on the same topic sees the envelope bytes, exactly as a consumer written against a broker's own client would, which is the body-interoperability change the specification requires the mode to be explicit about. A source in envelope mode reading a body that is not an envelope reports CodeCorruptMessage rather than handing over nonsense. That fetch hands over the deliveries before the corrupt one. The corrupt one goes back as a failed delivery, so the attempt limit and the dead-letter topic decide what becomes of it, and the ones the fetch claimed after it go back unseen, in their order and without spending an attempt.

    A sink in that mode refuses, before anything is stored, a message its own source could not decode: an identifier or a header key over 256 bytes, or any other field over the iqmq/wire decode limits, fails with CodeMessageTooLarge, and an identifier, header key or string property that is not valid UTF-8 fails with CodeCorruptMessage. Those are the codes the decode would have failed with, and the same ones driverkit.Envelope gives. Admitted, such a record would fail every fetch and go back to its lane each time, holding up a strictly ordered lane for good.

    Limits: only what the broker really knows is advertised, because the specification forbids an unadvertised limit from being read as a limit of zero.

    Limit Memory
    MaxMessageBytes the default topology's MaxMessageSize, when it has one
    MaxLease not advertised. This broker has no longest lease: Extend moves the deadline to at least now + d for any d, and the lane's scheduler is a list move rather than a timer per delivery. The topology's AckDeadline is the lease a delivery STARTS with, which the specification's Limits has no field for
    MaxBatchMessages, MaxBatchBytes, MaxHeaderCount, MaxHeaderBytes, MaxDelay not advertised: there is no limit

    Capabilities is constant per backend and cannot describe one topic, so a topic created with a different MaxMessageSize through the admin surface is enforced by the engine rather than by the core's pre-flight check.

    Byte accounting counts the key and the body, not the header storage: counting headers means walking the header set on every publication, and mq.Headers exposes no allocation-free size accessor. Snapshot.Bytes and the capacity bounds are therefore key-plus-body figures.

    Reconnect¶

    There is nothing to reconnect to, so there is no reconnect loop, no backoff and no Hooks.OnReconnect event from this driver. Client.Health() is Ready until the broker closes and then Closed; it is never Degraded.

    Faults.Reconnect() simulates what a reconnect does to a subscription: the assignment generation increments and every lease outstanding under the old one is revoked, so a settlement holding one reports ErrLeaseLost. The deliveries it revokes are not failures and do not consume an attempt.

    At-least-once window¶

    At-least-once is the default and the only delivery model this driver ships. The window in which a message can be delivered twice is the ordinary one: a delivery that was processed and whose acknowledgement did not reach the engine — because the lease expired first, because a rebalance revoked it, or because the consumer closed — is redelivered. Faults.DuplicateDelivery reproduces it deterministically (and is a documented no-op under a shared partition log, which hands each slot to each cursor exactly once by construction; Faults.Duplicates() reports which happened).

    FeatureExactlyOnceDelivery is not advertised, and it used to be. The root API defines it as "de-duplicated at-least-once delivery" — a consumer never sees one publication twice — and the paragraph above says plainly that this broker redelivers a delivery whose lease expired, so a consumer can. What the claim described was publication de-duplication: a publication carrying a dedup key is discarded if the topic has seen that key inside DedupWindow, in this process, and a restart forgets the window. That is FeatureDeduplication, which is still advertised as Conditional on TopologyConfig.DedupWindow. The window runs from the admission of the publication that carried the key, so a publication that waited for room under OverflowBlock for longer than the window still keeps its duplicates out for a whole window after it is admitted.

    ConfirmAccepted means the record is in every target lane. A process that dies after a publication returned loses the record: there is no durable boundary, and ConfirmDurable says so by failing.

    Config & DSN¶

    memory.Config is the struct. The zero value is usable and is the bounded, ordered configuration: one partition per subscription, 65,536 records per lane, a 30-second lease, the blocking overflow policy, the real clock, and the configured variant-family defaults.

    Two fields are deliberately asymmetric, so that "I did not set it" and "I want no limit" cannot be the same value:

    Field Zero Negative
    TopologyConfig.CapacityMessages 65,536 records per lane unbounded, which selects the chunked ready queue
    TopologyConfig.Partitions 1 lane, and therefore strict order GOMAXPROCS lanes
    TopologyConfig.AckDeadline 30 seconds leases never expire

    TopologyConfig.MaxInFlight and MaxDelayed are fields the engine does not have: it bounds the ready, in-flight and delayed records of a lane together through CapacityMessages. A non-zero value is refused at construction rather than ignored.

    EngineConfig pins one kind of every variant family: the ready queue, the fan-out architecture, the wait strategy, the scheduler, the key hash, the pools, the topic registry, the counters and the retry placement. The zero value is each family's Auto; Safe forces the mutex-backed reference kind of every family at once, which is the configuration that contains no lock-free data structure at all. The single-producer and single-consumer ready queues are refused: a lane is fed by any number of publishers and drained by any number of consumers.

    DSN, under memory:// and the alias mem://. A memory DSN names no host, user, path or fragment — there is nothing to connect to — and an unknown parameter is an error rather than a warning.

    memory://?partitions=8&capacity=100000&overflow=block&wildcards=true&autocreate=true
    Parameter Values
    autocreate true | false
    partitions a positive integer up to 1024, or auto for GOMAXPROCS
    capacity a positive integer, or unbounded
    capacitybytes, maxmessagesize, maxtotalmessages, maxtotalbytes positive integers
    kind queue | topic | stream
    overflow block | reject | dropnew | dropold
    fanout atomic | independent | besteffort | dropslow
    ordering relaxed | strict
    ackdeadline a Go duration, or never
    maxattempts a positive integer
    deadletter a topic name
    dedupwindow, retentionage Go durations
    retentionmessages, retentionbytes positive integers
    priority, wildcards, zeroonrelease true | false; priority=true is refused beside ordering=strict, because priorities reorder the lane strict ordering keeps
    engine safe

    memory.Register(reg) registers both schemes with a DSN registry. A driver exposes Register and never reaches for a global registry of its own.

    Known gotchas¶

    • Admin.DeleteTopic answers ErrUnsupported. The engine's registry has no removal: a topic exists for the life of the broker. A caller that wants a fresh one builds a fresh broker, which in process costs nothing. Closing every subscription and leaving the topic in the registry would be neither a delete nor a no-op, so it is not done.
    • Admin.Purge is an emulation and does not reach everything. A temporary consumer claims and settles every record the subscription has ready. It does not discard a record another consumer holds in flight — that record is not the subscription's to throw away while a lease is outstanding — nor one still waiting for its due time. What it discards is counted in Snapshot.Acked, for the same reason a drop is.
    • FeatureWildcardSubscribe is unsupported even though the engine has subject routing. SourceConfig carries no filter, so a wildcard subscription cannot be opened through the SPI; a topic may still be created with wildcards, and a delivery from one reports the resolved subject as its topic.
    • A delivery is borrowed until it is settled. Reading d.Body after d.Ack() is reading storage the engine may have released — and, with ZeroOnRelease, storage it has overwritten. d.Message.Clone() is the independent copy.
    • Publishing to a topic with no subscription is not an error. The record is discarded and released, which is an admission of nothing to nobody; Broker.Totals() reports it as discarded.
    • The default overflow policy blocks. A publisher that finds a full lane parks until room appears, its context ends or the publisher closes. That is the default and it is the right one for a test double, but it means a publication into a lane nobody is draining hangs rather than failing. OverflowReject is the fail-fast policy. A publication that could never fit is refused with ErrQueueFull instead of parked. Under FanoutAtomic, which FanoutAuto resolves to, that includes one whose records, one for each subscription, are more than MaxTotalMessages or MaxTotalBytes can hold. Under the fan-outs that admit a record wherever it fits (FanoutIndependent, FanoutBestEffort and FanoutDropSlow) the publisher waits until any one of its subscriptions could take the record, and the record could never fit only when none of them could ever hold it. Whichever overflow policy refuses such a publication, mq.IsRetryable reports the refusal as not retryable, because every retry meets the same refusal; OverflowDropNewest discards it instead. Its code is still CodeResourceExhausted, and callers that decide by the code alone read it as a condition that clears: the x/outbox relay treats it as the broker being down, so an ordered relay stops at such a row, and middleware.Retry, Transient and CircuitBreaker treat it as transient.
    • FanoutDropSlow drops as many records as a publication needs. A full subscription gives up its oldest records until the new one fits, which under a byte capacity can be several, and drops nothing when its leases leave no room for the record whatever it drops. Under an engine-wide limit the subscriptions that hold records give them up in turn, one each per pass, on every architecture. When that makes room for only some of the subscriptions taking the record, the ones that held nothing to drop take it first, so a subscription that keeps up is not refused while a slow one keeps its backlog. A shared partition log admits a publication to every subscription or to none, so there such a publication is refused and nothing is dropped for it. A record a lease gives back unsettled (a nack, an expired lease, a closed consumer) is one it may drop, and a publisher parked under OverflowBlock takes it then.
    • OverflowDropOldest shares the engine-wide limit across subscriptions. A lane makes room by discarding the record it would have served next. Under FanoutAtomic with an engine-wide limit, a publication that every subscription's lane together can make room for is admitted, and the lanes that hold records give them up in turn, one each per pass, for a lane that holds none (every record in flight, say). A shared partition log does the same. A publication the lanes cannot make room for even together is refused, and nothing is discarded for it.
    • Broker.Advance needs a clock the broker owns. It answers ErrUnsupported on a broker built with no Config.Clock or with a clock of the caller's own, because a fault that silently did nothing would make a test that relies on it pass for the wrong reason. NewTestClock is the clock it needs and NewForTest installs one.
    • Faults is broker-wide. A fault armed for "the next n" is armed for the next n of anything, on any topic.

    See the pinned client version and the module tests when evaluating this driver.
