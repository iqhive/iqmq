    Rebalance¶

    A rebalance is the broker taking a partition away from this process and giving it to another one, usually because a consumer joined or left. Everything in-flight on that partition is suddenly somebody else's problem, and the question is whether it is processed twice, processed once, or lost.

    This page is mostly about Kafka, because Kafka is where the portable API has to do the most work.

    The offset tracker¶

    Kafka commits a single offset per partition and means "everything before this is done". A consumer that processes messages concurrently completes them out of order, so the naive commit — the highest offset seen — would skip the ones still in flight.

    driverkit/offset is the tracker that makes this correct:

    • it marks delivered offsets as in-flight;
    • it commits only the highest contiguous completed offset plus one;
    • a failed or unresolved offset therefore blocks later commits rather than being skipped over;
    • it pauses the partition when the gap or the in-flight count exceeds its bounds, so an unbounded gap cannot become unbounded memory.

    The offset-tracker implementations and their benchmarks are under driverkit/offset. Compare them on your workload before changing the binding.

    The consequence to plan around: one stuck message stops the commit for its whole partition. The messages behind it are still processed — they are marked in-flight and completed — but the committed position does not advance past the gap, so a rebalance re-delivers them. That is the trade the specification asks for: duplicates rather than a silent skip.

    What the driver does on revocation¶

    In OnPartitionsRevoked, in this order:

    1. stop dispatching for the revoked partitions. No new handler starts on work that is about to belong to someone else.
    2. wait, bounded. Handlers already running get a chance to finish.
    3. commit the safe boundary synchronously. Still inside the callback, because after it returns the partition is gone and a commit is refused.
    4. invalidate the old generations. Every outstanding delivery for those partitions has its generation bumped, so a late settlement from a handler that finished after step 2 returns mq.ErrLeaseLost instead of settling an offset that now belongs to another consumer.

    Step 4 is what the generation token is for, and it is why a stale *mq.Delivery is safe to hold: it cannot settle the new owner's work.

    What you see¶

    Signal Meaning
    mq.ErrLeaseLost from a settlement the partition moved while your handler ran. Expected during a rebalance; the message will be redelivered.
    ConsumerStats.Redelivered jumping the new owner started from the last committed position
    the handler's context cancelled the bounded wait in step 2 expired

    None of the three is an error to alert on individually. A rate of them that does not settle down after a deploy is: that is a consumer group that cannot stop rebalancing, usually because a handler is slower than the session timeout.

    Ordering across a rebalance¶

    Order within a key survives only if the key's partition does not move, and a rebalance moves partitions. The cooperative-sticky balancer minimises the movement — it is the driver's default for exactly this reason — but it does not eliminate it.

    If your requirement is that a key is never processed out of order even across a rebalance, the ordering has to come from the broker rather than from the consumer: a FIFO group (SQS FIFO), a Key_Shared subscription (Pulsar), or a partition you never move.

    The other brokers¶

    • SQS has no rebalance: every consumer takes from the same queue, and a visibility timeout is the only thing that transfers a message.
    • JetStream and Pub/Sub redistribute by lease expiry rather than by a group protocol, so the "rebalance" is just the redelivery path and mq.WithLeaseExtension is the lever.
    • Redis Streams reassigns through the pending-entries list: the mandatory XAUTOCLAIM reclaim loop takes over entries idle past the ack deadline, and the delivery count it returns becomes Delivery.Attempt.
    • RabbitMQ moves a consumer rather than a partition; the driver owns bounded reconnection and topology restoration.

    Each driver page's Reconnect and At-least-once window sections state its own version.

    What is not tested yet¶

    What is tested: TestCriticalReconnectRestoresTheTopology and the drivers' own critical tests, in-process, in every make ci. One of them is rare-flaky under host load and is recorded as such rather than retried until green.
