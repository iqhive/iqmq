    Acks and leases¶

    What happens between "your handler received this" and "the broker will not send it again".

    That diagram is the runner's actual control flow — run.go's settle, fail, retry, exhaust and deadLetter — rather than the obvious version, and four of its details are worth reading twice.

    A lease is a deadline, not a lock¶

    When a broker hands you a message it usually hands you a deadline with it: visibility timeout (SQS), ack deadline (Pub/Sub, JetStream), in-flight timeout (NSQ). Past that instant the broker considers the delivery abandoned and gives the message to somebody else — while your handler is still running.

    Delivery.LeaseExpires is when that happens, where the driver knows. Delivery.Extend(by) pushes it out. mq.WithLeaseExtension(d) asks the runner to do it for you while a handler is slow, and mq.WithLeaseDeadline() narrows the handler's own context to the lease so that a handler cannot keep working past the point at which its work no longer counts.

    WithLeaseExtension takes a lead — how long before the deadline to ask — and there is one scheduler and one timer for the whole runner, never a timer per delivery. What it asks the broker for is the lease's own length, and the lead decides when: an extension is requested a lead before the deadline it is renewing.

    A lead longer than the lease cannot be honoured, and the runner extends more often instead of asking for more time. Nothing can buy 30 seconds of notice out of a 10-second visibility timeout, and asking the broker for 30 seconds would be refused outright on the three drivers that cap an extension (drivers/sqs, drivers/gcppubsub, drivers/nsq) — so the runner keeps the delivery alive on shorter notice rather than losing it altogether. The floor is half the lease: the deadline is never less than half a lease away, and the lead is honoured exactly whenever it is under that.

    A refused extension is not retried. The lease is either gone — in which case the broker has the message and the settlement that follows says so — or the driver refused, and asking again on the same interval turns one broken delivery into a busy loop. Your handler is not cancelled either: it finds out at its own settlement, and the OnSettle hook fires for the failed extension with kind SettleExtend if you want to know sooner.

    Two honest limits. A driver with no DeadlineExtender cannot extend at all, and Extend returns ErrUnsupported — the matrix's ExtendLease row says which. And extension is a race you can lose: the broker may have already redelivered, in which case the settlement that follows returns ErrLeaseLost rather than quietly settling somebody else's delivery.

    The generation token is why a stale handle is safe¶

    Every delivery carries a generation. A *Delivery you captured in a closure and settle after the message has been redelivered settles nothing and returns ErrLeaseLost — it cannot reach the new delivery, even though the runner has pooled and reused the struct underneath. This is the invariant that makes buffer reuse safe to have at all.

    The dead-letter order is the whole point¶

    Publish to the dead-letter destination, confirm it, and only then ack the original. A publication that is refused or ambiguous leaves the original unacked and requeues it, and increments a counter of its own.

    A runner that acked first would destroy the message in exactly the case the dead-letter topic exists for. A runner that acked after a publication it could not confirm would do the same, less often and less reproducibly.

    The five provenance headers a dead-lettered message carries:

    Header Content
    iqmq-origin-topic the topic it was consumed from
    iqmq-origin-subscription the subscription that consumed it
    iqmq-attempts the failed attempt, in decimal; absent where the broker counts none
    iqmq-failure the error the handler reported, rendered by its own Error
    iqmq-first-delivered-at when it was first delivered — written only when this delivery is the first

    The last row is a deliberate absence. A redelivery may reach a different process entirely and nothing in the SPI carries a first-delivery time back, so a missing header says "not known" and a header holding this attempt's receive time would say something false.

    On a driver whose broker moves the message itself — mq.WithNativeDeadLetter() over Delivery.Nack(mq.NackDeadLetter()) — there is no publication, no confirmation over d.Nack(mq.NackDeadLetter()) — there is no publication, no confirmationwindow, and no provenance headers, because nothing on that path writes a over d.Nack(mq.NackDeadLetter()) — there is no publication, no confirmation window, and no provenance headers, because nothing on that path writes amessage. What the destination receives is what the broker put there.

    Who settles¶

    mq.WithSettlement(mq.SettlementManual) hands settlement to your handler entirely: the runner dispatches and then leaves the delivery alone, whatever the handler returns. Under the default mq.SettlementAuto the handler may still settle for itself, and is not overruled — the runner checks, and every settlement it performs tolerates ErrAlreadySettled, because the handler may settle on another goroutine and win the race with that check.
